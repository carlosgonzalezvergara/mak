1. Overview

M.A.K. is a browser-based diagramming tool designed for syntactic analysis within the framework of Systemic Functional Linguistics (SFL). It allows users to build constituency tree diagrams interactively, assigning multiple rows of labels per node to reflect the simultaneous functional layers characteristic of SFL analysis.

M.A.K. is a manual assistant: the user makes all analytical decisions (choosing where to divide constituents, what labels to assign, and how to organise the tree) while the program handles hierarchical visualisation, label management, and image export.

The tool runs entirely in the browser. No data is uploaded to any server; all work is stored locally in the browser or saved as downloadable files.

2. The interface

M.A.K. uses a split layout with two areas:

  • Left panel — a narrow sidebar containing the logo, the ✦ New Diagram and Manual buttons, file operations (Save Project, Load Project, Download as Image), a Labels section, and a collapsible quick-reference guide.
  • Canvas — the main area where the tree diagram is displayed and edited. When no diagram is active, the canvas shows a placeholder message.

Controls whose effect depends on what you are doing show that in their appearance. The label formatting buttons under Labels, for instance, are always in view but dimmed and inactive until you click a label to edit it (see section 6b).

Canvas controls

Two floating controls sit over the canvas itself, so they do not take up panel space. They are dimmed when idle and become fully opaque when you hover over them, and neither appears in an exported image.

  • Top right — Undo, Redo, and the zoom controls. Use [−] and [+] to adjust the view, or click the percentage indicator to reset to 100%. The zoom range is 60%–200% in 20% increments.
  • Top left — the auto-save indicator, showing whether M.A.K. is saving or how long ago the last save was made.

Auto-save

M.A.K. automatically saves your work to the browser's local storage every few seconds. If you close the tab or browser and return later, M.A.K. shows a Restore previous session dialog indicating how long ago the backup was made and offering two options: Continue (loads the backup so you can pick up where you left off) or Discard (clears the backup and starts with a fresh canvas). This dialog requires an explicit choice — it cannot be dismissed by clicking outside. Press Enter to continue or Esc to discard. Auto-saved data is stored locally only; nothing is uploaded.

Note: Auto-saved data may be lost if you clear your browser's site data. For permanent storage, use the Save Project function to download a .mak file.

3. Creating a new diagram

  1. Click ✦ New Diagram in the left panel.
  2. A dialog box appears. Type the expression you want to analyse in the text field.
  3. If you need IPA characters, click the t͡ʃ IPA button to open the IPA character picker. Characters are inserted at the cursor position in the text field.
  4. Choose the Word separation option below the text field, or check that the remembered one still matches the language of this expression. Each option carries a short description of what it does, and the three are covered in section 4.
  5. Click Analyse (or press Enter). The root node is created with the full expression.

From that point, your work consists of dividing constituents, assigning labels, and — when necessary — adding extra nodes or editing node text.

The IPA character picker

The picker is organised into categories: vowels, plosives, nasals, fricatives, approximants, affricates, clicks, suprasegmentals, diacritics (voicing, place, manner, secondary), tones, and bracketing symbols. Click any character to insert it into the expression field. The picker closes when you dismiss the dialog or toggle it off.

Undo and redo

M.A.K. maintains a history of up to 50 states. Use the Undo and Redo buttons in the floating control at the top right of the canvas, or the keyboard shortcuts (see section 11), to step back and forth through your editing history.

4. Automatic word separation

In many languages, a single orthographic word may correspond to more than one grammatical word. For example, Spanish díselo contains a verb stem and two clitic pronouns (dí + se + lo), and English don't is a contraction of do + not. Since syntactic analysis typically operates on grammatical words, M.A.K. includes an automatic word-separation system that detects these cases and offers to expand them before the tree is created.

This process has two layers: one that applies silently (unambiguous cases) and one that asks for user confirmation (ambiguous cases).

Choosing the language

The rules for Spanish and English are not interchangeable, so M.A.K. applies only the set that corresponds to the language you select. The choice is made in the Word separation control of the ✦ New Diagram dialog, alongside the expression you are about to analyse, and it is remembered between sessions. There is no default: on a first visit no option is preselected and Analyse stays inactive until you pick one, since the appropriate rules cannot be guessed from the expression.

OptionWhat it applies
SpanishThe Spanish contractions and fused pronouns, plus the enclitic-pronoun dialog.
EnglishThe English contractions, plus the ambiguous 's / 'd dialog.
NoneNothing. The expression is used exactly as typed.
Note: M.A.K. has separation rules for Spanish and English only. If you work with any other language, choose None, which leaves every expression untouched. Selecting a language that does not match the expression produces unwanted separations: with Spanish selected, for instance, English dime would be offered as di + me, and Al would be expanded to a el.

Silent expansions

The following transformations are applied automatically when you click Analyse, provided the corresponding language is selected. They require no user action.

Spanish:

  • del → de el
  • al → a el
  • conmigo → con migo, contigo → con tigo, consigo → con sigo

English:

  • Irregular negatives: won't → will not, can't → can not, shan't → shall not, ain't → am not
  • Regular negatives: don't → do not, doesn't → does not, isn't → is not, etc.
  • Unambiguous verb contractions: I'm → I am, you're → you are, they'll → they will, I've → I have, let's → let us, wanna → want to

Interactive: Spanish enclitic pronouns

When M.A.K. detects words that appear to contain enclitic pronouns (e.g. díselo, haciéndolo, ponme), it shows a confirmation dialog before creating the tree. Each candidate is displayed with a checkbox and a suggested separation:

☑ díselo → dí se lo

☑ haciéndolo → haciendo lo

All candidates are checked by default. Uncheck any item you do not want to separate, then click Confirm to proceed. Click Back to return to the input field.

The detection logic examines each word by stripping potential clitic sequences from the end (me, te, se, le, lo, la, nos, os, les, los, las) and checking whether the remaining stem has the shape of a verb form: an infinitive ending in -r, a gerund ending in -ndo, an imperative with a stress-marking accent, or one of the short imperative stems (di, da, ve, haz, pon, sal, ten, ven). A list of exclusions prevents false positives from common words such as modelo, consuelo, cielo, ejemplo, or aquellos.

Interactive: English ambiguous contractions

Two English contractions are genuinely ambiguous and require user intervention:

  • 's — can represent is, has, or a possessive (no separation needed).
  • 'd — can represent would or had.

When M.A.K. detects any of these, it displays a disambiguation dialog with a drop-down menu for each occurrence:

she's →

he'd →

Select the appropriate expansion for each item, then click Apply and analyse. If you prefer to keep all contractions unexpanded, click Skip.

Note: The word-separation system only runs when a new diagram is created. It does not affect text that is later edited manually within individual nodes, and changing the setting does not alter a diagram that already exists.

5. Division mechanics

Dividing a node

To divide a constituent into smaller parts, click on a tinted node box (the node must contain more than one word). M.A.K. enters division mode and displays the words with clickable cut bars between them.

  • Click one or more grey bars between words to mark the boundaries between constituents. Selected bars turn dark.
  • Click Divide to generate the child nodes.
  • Click Cancel to exit division mode without making changes.

Consolidating a node

If a node contains more than one orthographic word but should be treated as a single syntactic unit (e.g. a phrasal verb or a proper name), you can consolidate it to prevent division. Hover over the node and click the ∪ button in the upper-left corner. The node will no longer be divisible and will be displayed with a white background, like a terminal node. To reverse this, click the ÷ button that replaces it.

Undoing a division

To reverse a division, click the ↩ button. It removes all children of that node at once, together with any labels they carried, and gives the node back as a single undivided box with its segment inside, ready to be analysed again. To remove one branch and keep the others, see below instead.

Where the button sits depends on whether the node shows a text box, because a node whose text is hidden has no box to hang controls on:

NodePosition of ↩
Text visibleTo the right of the text box. Visible without hovering.
Text hiddenTop right of the label stack, opposite the delete button. Appears on hover.
Root node, text hiddenOn the label row below it, since the root displays its labels underneath.

On a node with no children whose text is hidden, the same button restores the text instead of collapsing a division. Every hidden node therefore has a way back.

Deleting a single branch

Hover over a node and click the red bin button at the top left of its label stack. This deletes that node and everything below it, while its sisters keep their own labels and structure. The button appears on every node except the root, and only one is shown at a time: the one belonging to the node your cursor is actually on.

Before you click, hovering over the button outlines the whole branch that will be removed and fades it out, so you can see the exact extent of the deletion. If the branch contains descendants or labels you have written, a confirmation dialog then states how many nodes and how many labels will be lost. Leaf nodes with no labels are deleted without confirmation. Deleting the last remaining child of a node returns that node to its undivided state, exactly as ↩ would.

Note: Deleting a branch does not corrupt the analysis. The children of a node are not required to reconstruct its text: extra nodes and elision markers (see section 7) already break that correspondence by design, and division always reads a node's own text rather than recomputing it from its children.

Hiding node text

In a systemic functional diagram you rarely want the segment repeated at every level, since the labels carry the analysis. Hover over a node and click the crossed-out eye in the upper-right corner to hide its text while keeping its labels and children intact. The button appears only on nodes containing more than one word, which are the ones that can still be divided; it is not shown on single-word terminal nodes, on consolidated nodes, or on manually inserted ones.

Note: Hiding is not deleting. M.A.K. keeps the segment inside the node, which is what allows ↩ to give it back to you later. In files saved before this behaviour existed the text was genuinely lost, and in those cases ↩ rebuilds the segment by joining the leaves of the branch, skipping inserted nodes and elision markers.

To bring a hidden text back without undoing anything, use the ↩ button described above: on a node with no children it restores the text rather than collapsing a division.

6. The label system

M.A.K.'s label system is designed for SFL analysis, where a single constituent may need to be labelled simultaneously in several functional layers (e.g. transitivity, mood, theme).

Label placement

  • The root node displays its labels below the text box.
  • All other nodes display their labels above the text box.
  • When the root node contains text, a vertical connector links it to its lower label stack. If the root text is cleared, that connector is hidden.

Multiple label rows

Each node supports between 1 and 5 rows of labels. Non-root nodes are created with 2 rows by default; the root node starts with 1.

  • Hover over a label stack and click the + button to add a new row (up to 5).
  • Click the small × at the bottom right of a label box to remove that row. It appears only when a node has more than one row, since every node keeps at least one. This button removes a label row, not the node: to delete a node and its branch, see section 5.

Editing labels

Click on any label to enter edit mode. Type the desired value and press Enter or click elsewhere to confirm. You can use Tab to advance to the next label and Shift+Tab to go back.

Formatting within labels

Labels support inline text formatting using simple markup. You can type the markup directly, or apply it with the buttons in the Label Formatting panel (see section 6b).

MarkupResultExample
**text**Actor**Actor**
*text*Theme*Theme*
_text_Participant1Participant_1_
__text__NP2NP__2__
%text%Theme%Theme%
~text~Given~Given~

The same markup set is used in Telperion, so formatting habits transfer between the two tools. All six styles are preserved in the exported PNG image.

6a. The label catalogue

M.A.K. allows each user to define a personal label catalogue — a set of labels that become available as quick-select options whenever a label field is being edited. This is particularly useful in teaching contexts or when working with a fixed analytical framework, as it reduces typing and minimises inconsistencies across diagrams.

Organising the catalogue into groups

Because a node can carry several label rows at once, and each row usually belongs to a different layer of analysis, a single undivided list makes the quick-select menu noisier the more complete your catalogue becomes. To avoid this, start a line with # to open a named group:

# Transitivity
Actor, Process, Goal, Circumstance
# Unit class
nominal group, verbal group, prepositional phrase

The quick-select menu then shows your labels under those headings, and as you type it filters within each group and hides the ones with no matches. Labels written before the first # simply appear without a heading, so a catalogue with no groups at all behaves exactly as it always did and needs no conversion.

Setting up the catalogue

Click Set Up Labels in the Labels section of the left panel. A dialog box will open with the following elements:

  • A text area where you can type or paste your labels, separated by commas, line breaks, or both.
  • A live preview below the text area that shows how the labels will look as selectable pills, updating as you type.
  • A Save button to confirm and apply the catalogue.
  • A Cancel button to close the dialog without making changes.
  • A Clear all button (shown only when a catalogue already exists) to delete all stored labels.

Example input (comma-separated):

NG, NV, Nadj, Nadv, Nprep, S, P, O, Cs, Co, A

Example input (line-separated):

Actor
Process
Goal
Beneficiary
Circumstance

Both formats — and combinations of them — are accepted. Leading and trailing spaces around each label are stripped automatically.

Using the catalogue while editing

Once a catalogue is active, a dropdown panel appears automatically whenever you click on a label field to edit it. The panel displays all catalogue labels as clickable pills. Click any pill to insert that label immediately — the field closes and the value is confirmed.

As you type in the label field, the pill list narrows in real time to show only those labels that contain the characters you have typed (case-insensitive filtering). This makes it easy to locate the right label in a large catalogue.

The dropdown also includes a clear label option at the top, which empties the field in one click without having to select and delete the text manually.

You can always type freely in the label field, whether or not a catalogue is loaded. The catalogue is an aid, not a constraint — any custom text can still be entered directly.

Persistence across sessions

The catalogue is stored in the browser's local storage under the key mak-labels. It persists automatically across sessions: if you close the tab or browser and return later, your catalogue will be available without any further action. The button in the panel reflects the current state, showing Labels (N) — where N is the number of labels — when a catalogue is loaded.

Note: The catalogue is browser-specific. If you use M.A.K. on a different browser or device, you will need to re-enter your labels. Clearing browser site data will also erase the stored catalogue.

Saving and loading the catalogue as a file

Although the catalogue persists automatically in the browser, you may want to back it up, share it with others, or transfer it to a different device or browser. The Label Catalogue dialog includes two buttons for this purpose, located below the main action buttons:

  • Save to file — downloads the current catalogue as a plain-text file (mak-labels.txt), with one label per line. This button is disabled when the catalogue is empty.
  • Load from file — opens a file picker and loads a previously saved .txt catalogue. The contents of the file are placed directly into the text area, where you can review or edit them before clicking Save to apply.

The .txt format is deliberately simple: you can open the file in any text editor, add or remove labels, and reload it into M.A.K. The parser accepts both comma-separated and line-separated formats, so either layout in the file will be read correctly.

6b. Label formatting and symbols

Under Labels in the left panel there is a row of formatting buttons and an α Symbols list, giving button-based access to text formatting and to the special characters used in systemic functional notation. They act on the label you are editing, so they are dimmed and inactive until you click a label to enter edit mode. The symbol list is the one exception: it can be opened and browsed at any time, since consulting the inventory is useful even when there is nothing to insert into.

Note: Clicking any of these controls does not close the label you are editing. You can apply several styles and insert several characters in succession without leaving edit mode, and the text cursor stays where you left it.

Formatting buttons

Each button applies the corresponding markup described in section 6. If text is selected, the markup wraps the selection and the selection is preserved, so you can apply a second style on top of the first. If nothing is selected, the markup is inserted with the cursor placed between the two markers, ready for you to type.

6c. Telling the delete controls apart

Three controls remove or hide something, and they are deliberately different in shape, position and colour so they are not confused with one another.

ControlPositionWhat it does
Small × Bottom right of a label box Removes that label row only. Nothing else changes.
Crossed-out eye Top right of a text box Hides the node’s text. Reversible, and the segment is kept.
Red bin Top left of a label stack Deletes the whole branch below and including that node.

Red is reserved for the only one of the three that destroys structure. The other two use the accent colour, and both can be undone with ↩ or with Undo.

7. Extra nodes and elision

Adding an extra child node

Once a node has been divided into children, you can insert additional child nodes. This is useful for representing elided or implicit constituents. Hover over the parent node and click the +1 button at the bottom of the node box.

  1. A prompt will ask for the text of the new node (this can be left empty).
  2. A position picker dialog appears, letting you choose where to insert the new node: At the beginning, At the end, or Between "X" and "Y".

Extra nodes are marked internally as inserted nodes and behave like any other node — they can receive labels and be further divided.

8. Editing node text

M.A.K. allows you to edit the text of any node directly in the tree, without needing to undo and redo divisions. Hover over a node and click the ✎ button in the bottom-right corner.

The node's text becomes an editable input field. Type the new text and press Enter or click outside to confirm. This is useful for correcting typos, adjusting word boundaries, or modifying individual words after the initial division.

The root node adjusts its width to the text it contains. Other text-bearing nodes use a fixed box width; if the text is longer than the box, only the initial portion is displayed followed by ellipsis. This affects display only and does not alter the underlying text used for division.

9. File management

M.A.K. uses files with the .mak extension to save and load diagrams. These are JSON files that preserve the entire tree structure, all labels, and the original expression.

Save Project

Click Save Project in the left panel (or press Ctrl+S / ⌘+S) to open the save dialog. The dialog suggests a filename generated automatically from the first words of the expression; you can edit it freely or accept the default. The .mak extension is added automatically. Press Enter to confirm or Esc (or click outside the dialog) to cancel.

Load Project

Click Load Project in the left panel and select a .mak file from your computer.

Labels travel with the file

A saved .mak file also carries your label catalogue, so an exercise can be shared complete: a teacher can send students a single file with the labels for the unit already in it, and your own catalogue follows a project between computers. Files saved with earlier versions of M.A.K. simply have no catalogue in them and open exactly as before.

When you open a file that includes labels, M.A.K. only interrupts you if something could be lost:

  • If your catalogue is empty, the labels from the file are loaded silently.
  • If you already have all of them, nothing happens.
  • If the file brings labels you do not have, a dialog lists them and offers three choices: Add the new ones (keeps your catalogue and appends what is missing), Replace mine (uses the file’s catalogue instead of yours), or Keep mine (changes nothing). The diagram opens in all three cases.

10. Download as Image

M.A.K. exports the diagram as a high-resolution PNG image, suitable for insertion into documents, slides, or academic papers. Click Download as Image in the left panel to open the export dialog.

As with saving, the dialog suggests a filename generated from the first words of the expression. Below the filename, a Background selector lets you choose between:

  • White (default): produces a PNG with a solid white background. This is the safest choice for embedding the image in documents, slides, or printed materials.
  • Transparent: produces a PNG with no background. Useful when you want to compose the diagram over a coloured slide background or layer it with other graphics.

Press Enter to confirm or Esc (or click outside the dialog) to cancel.

All label styling is rendered into the image: bold, italic, small caps, subscript, superscript and underline, together with any IPA or systemic functional characters used in the labels. The editing controls that appear on hover (add, remove, clear, edit buttons) are hidden during export and do not appear in the file.
